Disease areas

Diseases that take central vision

We focus on degenerative diseases of the retina, where better long-acting treatments could protect sight for millions of people.

The burden

A large and growing need

Age-related macular degeneration is a leading cause of severe visual impairment and blindness in the United States.2 In the US, diabetic eye disease is the leading cause of vision loss among working-age adults.3

Focus areas

The diseases we study

Wet age-related macular degeneration

What happens

Abnormal blood vessels grow under the retina and leak fluid and blood. Central vision can decline quickly.

Treatment today

Anti-VEGF biologics injected into the eye, typically every one to four months, often for years.

Where peptides could help

Longer-acting, precisely targeted peptides could reduce how often patients need injections and reach pathways beyond VEGF.

Dry AMD and geographic atrophy

What happens

In advanced dry AMD, called geographic atrophy, patches of cells in the macula die and the patches grow over time.

Treatment today

Approved complement inhibitors slow lesion growth but do not stop it or restore vision, and they are given by injection into the eye.

Where peptides could help

Peptides could block disease-driving protein interactions that existing drugs do not reach.

Diabetic macular edema and diabetic retinopathy

What happens

High blood sugar damages small blood vessels in the retina. Fluid leaking into the macula causes diabetic macular edema.

Treatment today

Anti-VEGF injections, steroid implants and laser treatment, depending on the stage of disease.

Where peptides could help

Longer-acting molecules aimed at inflammation and vascular-leak pathways.
