Science

Peptides built for the hardest targets in the eye

Many of the proteins that drive retinal disease act through large, flat surfaces. We design peptides to bind them.

The challenge

Why so many retinal targets are still untreated

Proteins often work by binding to each other across wide, shallow contact surfaces. Small-molecule drugs need a deep pocket to hold on to, so these protein–protein interactions are hard to block.

Antibodies and other biologics can reach some of them. In the eye, they are injected into the vitreous on a recurring schedule, which is a burden for patients, caregivers and clinics.

Peptides sit between the two. They are large enough to cover a flat protein surface and small enough to make by chemical synthesis and to engineer for the eye.
