Mission

We design peptide and mini-protein medicines for the diseases that take central vision, starting from what patients need and working back to the molecule.

  1. i.

    Preserve central vision

    The measure that matters is what patients can still do: read, drive, recognize a face.

  2. ii.

    Longer between doses

    Today’s biologic treatments mean injections into the eye every few weeks or months. We are designing for longer intervals.

  3. iii.

    Reach new targets

    Peptides and mini-proteins can grip the large, flat protein surfaces behind retinal disease that small molecules miss, while staying far smaller than an antibody.

  4. iv.

    Built to be made

    Stability, delivery to the retina and manufacturability are design constraints from the first sequence, not problems to solve late.

The macula

A few millimetres of retina carry the vision we read, drive and see faces with.

Science

From a protein surface to a peptide worth making

Structure-based design, docking and molecular dynamics, for peptides and mini-proteins alike. Each step narrows thousands of candidates to the few worth testing in the lab.

01 · Target

Start from the surface

Many proteins behind retinal disease act through large, flat contact surfaces. We start by mapping the surface a binder has to cover.

02 · Design

Search sequence space

We generate candidate peptide and mini-protein sequences and refine them one residue at a time, guided by energy scoring and machine learning.

03 · Docking

Dock and score thousands of poses

The designs worth keeping settle into the same low-energy pose again and again, at the bottom of the funnel.

04 · Simulation

Simulate before we synthesize

Molecular dynamics tests whether a bound design holds its pose, and alanine scanning shows which residues carry the binding. Designs that pass go to partner labs.

Why it matters

Central vision is how we read, use a phone and recognize the people we love.

Age-related macular degeneration and diabetic eye disease damage the macula. We want to protect central vision for longer, with fewer injections along the way.

How AMD takes central vision

Scroll to see how vision can change as the disease advances.

  1. HealthySharp central vision. Print, faces and fine detail are clear.
  2. Early AMDUsually no symptoms yet, which is why regular eye exams matter.
  3. Intermediate AMDSome people notice mild blurriness in their central vision.
  4. Late AMDStraight lines can look wavy, and a blurry area near the center may grow or become blank spots.

Focus

Diseases that take central vision

196Mpeople worldwide projected to have age-related macular degeneration in 20201
288Mprojected by 2040, as populations age1
Neovascular

Wet AMD

Leaky blood vessels under the retina damage central vision. Treatment means repeated injections into the eye.

Atrophic

Dry AMD and geographic atrophy

Cells in the macula slowly die off. Current treatments slow the damage but do not stop it.

Vascular

Diabetic eye disease

Damaged retinal vessels leak fluid into the macula and blur central vision.

Progress

Where we are

We are in computational design today. Each stage ends in a go or no-go decision before the next one starts.

  1. Stage 1 Now

    Computational design

    Design, docking and molecular dynamics, with in-silico checks for selectivity and developability.

    Goal: a ranked lead series

  2. Stage 2

    Lab validation

    Synthesis with a manufacturing partner, then binding, cell and stability tests in serum and vitreous.

    Goal: a confirmed binder

  3. Stage 3

    Preclinical proof of concept

    How long a design stays in the eye after injection, and whether it works in a disease model.

    Goal: durable exposure and an efficacy signal

  4. Stage 4

    Partnering

    Co-development or licensing with retina-focused pharma and biotech, alongside non-dilutive funding.

    Goal: a partnered program

22

customer discovery interviews through NJIT’s I-Corps program in 2026, with drug discovery scientists, licensing leads, regulatory and manufacturing scientists, and retina specialists.

What we heard

  • Hard targets are the gap

    Scientists named protein–protein interaction targets that small molecules cannot reach, and antibodies that penetrate the retina poorly, as the top problem.

  • Durability matters to patients

    Retina specialists told us a longer-lasting option would change how well patients keep up with treatment.

  • Manufacturing decides deals

    Industry weighed cost of goods, scale-up and clean IP as heavily as dosing, so we design for synthesis from the first sequence.
